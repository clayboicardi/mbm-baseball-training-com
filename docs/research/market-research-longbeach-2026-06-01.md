## Executive summary

1. **MBM is already publicly listed at $36/lesson on TeachMe.To** (at Heartwell Park, tagged "Best Price"), while the draft site says $45/30-min. **Reconcile this** — inconsistent public pricing undercuts trust. ([TeachMe.To](https://teachme.to/browse/baseball/california/long-beach), 2026-06-01)
2. **Base 30-min rate ($45) is competitive.** Long Beach independent coaches cluster **$30–$64** per lesson (TeachMe.To Heartwell Park avg **$52**; CoachUp Long Beach floor **$45**; Athletes Untapped from **$30**). $45 sits mid-pack and credible.
3. **The 60-min rate ($65) is underpriced relative to the value ladder and to facilities.** It's only **$20 over the 30-min** (weak ladder) and well under facility 60-min rates (academies/CA facilities run **$90–$135**). Room to raise to **$75–$85**, or keep $65 as a deliberate value anchor — a decision flag, not a bug.
4. **The +$100 video-analysis add-on is mispriced HIGH — this is the clearest fix.** Comparable CA video add-ons are **$40 (video/photo) to $50 (Blast Motion)**; a full *standalone* analysis product runs ~**$199**. A +$100 add-on costs **more than the 30-min lesson itself** — a conversion killer. Recommend **$35–$40 add-on** and/or bundling a quick clip into sessions. ([Diamond Kids](https://www.diamondkidsbaseball.com/dkbpricing) · [Antonelli Baseball](https://www.antonellibaseball.com/videoanalysis), 2026-06-01)
5. **The Elite $1,500/season package is reasonable — lean conservative.** 30×60-min at the one-off $65 = $1,950, so $1,500 is a ~23% bulk discount + extras. It **undercuts the local premium academy** (d'Arnaud Athletics, **$1,999/yr** membership) while offering true 1-on-1. Main risk is the **$1,500 lump-sum ask** — add a payment plan / monthly framing.
6. **A free first lesson is market-standard, not a differentiator-by-itself.** TeachMe.To's model is literally "first lesson free"; CoachUp offers a 100% money-back first session. **Keep it** as the conversion hook, but don't over-claim it as unique.
7. **There's no mid-tier between one-off and a $1,500 commitment.** 10-packs are the local norm (d'Arnaud sells 10-packs; general market offers 6/10-packs). **Add a ~10-session package** as an on-ramp.
8. **MBM's open lane = premium-personal-affordable.** The market splits into MLB-credentialed *academies* (d'Arnaud, The Cage, On Deck) selling memberships/classes/tech, and *indie coaches* selling cheap per-session reps. MBM can own **"20+ yrs / former semi-pro, true 1-on-1, develop-the-whole-player — without academy prices."** (A credentialed indie locally, Justin Hall, commands **$142/lesson** — proof MBM is *underpricing his experience*.)

## Verdicts on the three open pricing questions

### Q1 — Is +$100 video analysis on a $45/$65 session in line with the market? **NO — it's ~2× too high. (Confidence: HIGH on direction)**
Comparable CA video add-ons are **$40–$50**; a full *standalone* analysis product is ~**$199**. A $100 add-on on a $45 lesson costs **more than the lesson itself** and nearly equals the 60-min — parents will not opt in.
**Fix:** price a **$35–$40 video add-on** ("Swing/Mechanics Video Breakdown"), and/or **include a quick slow-mo clip + feedback** in the 60-min session as a differentiator. Reserve a deeper annotated breakdown as a **$75–$99 standalone**. *Credibility note:* if MBM has no Rapsodo/Blast/HitTrax, sell it as "coach's-eye slow-motion video," not "data analytics," to stay honest vs. tech-equipped academies.

### Q2 — Is Elite ($1,500/season, ~$50/session for 30×60-min) priced right? **YES — reasonable, slightly conservative. (Confidence: MEDIUM)**
At the one-off $65, 30×60-min = **$1,950**, so $1,500 is a **~23% bulk discount** plus included extras — and it **undercuts the local premium academy** (d'Arnaud, $1,999/yr) while offering true 1-on-1 instead of academy classes. Good positioning.
**Adjust:** (a) **Add a payment plan / monthly framing** (e.g. 3×$525, or ~$275/mo × 6, or a "$249/mo membership") — the $1,500 lump sum is the real barrier, and academies sell monthly ($149/mo). (b) If the 60-min base rises to $80, recompute — the discount only looks stronger. (c) Value the "included video" at the *corrected* add-on price, not $100.

### Q3 — Are $45 (30-min) / $65 (60-min) competitive for an independent coach here? **30-min YES; 60-min UNDERPRICED. (Confidence: HIGH)**
$45/30-min sits right at the local indie median (TeachMe.To Heartwell avg $52; CoachUp floor $45). $65/60-min is **only $20 over the 30-min** (broken value ladder) and far below facility 60-min ($90–$135).
**Fix:** raise 60-min to **$75–$85** (still beats facilities, fixes the ladder), *or* keep $65 as a deliberate value play and market it as such. **And reconcile the $36 TeachMe.To listing with the $45 site rate.**

## Positioning gaps & differentiation recommendations

**The market is barbelled:** MLB-credentialed **academies** (d'Arnaud, The Cage, On Deck, Armory) sell memberships + tech + facility; **indie coaches** sell cheap per-session reps on marketplaces. Both ends are crowded; the **"experienced, personal, develops the whole player — without academy prices"** middle is open.

**Gaps MBM can own:**
1. **True 1-on-1 vs. the academy "class/membership" model.** Lead with undivided attention and a custom plan — the thing $1,999/yr academies *can't* give every kid.
2. **Credentialed but affordable.** 20+ yrs + former semi-pro is a real story. Justin Hall ($142) proves credentials command 2–3× locally — **MBM is underpricing his experience** at $36–$45. The free first lesson lets parents de-risk the higher-than-marketplace value.
3. **Develop the whole player / mental game + character.** The tagline ("Train with purpose. Play with confidence. Compete with passion.") already points here. Academies emphasize mechanics/tech; own **confidence, baseball IQ, and the mental side** as the through-line.
4. **Personal brand: Coach Myles, #11.** Indie marketplace listings are faceless ("Steve C.", "Coach Brandon"). A real name, face, story, and IG presence (@coach_berniard_mendez_) is a differentiator the platforms can't replicate.
5. **Accessible video analysis** — *if priced right* ($35–$40). Most indies don't offer it; it signals seriousness without academy pricing.
6. **Local & flexible** — Heartwell Park, flexible scheduling. (Scheduling flexibility is explicitly praised in competitor reviews.)

## Parent decision factors (from competitor reviews)

In priority order, drawn from review language across the comp set:
1. **Coach rapport & positivity** — "my son's favorite time of the week"; motivation and enjoyment keep kids coming back. ([lessons.com review snippet](https://lessons.com/ca/long-beach/baseball-training/))
2. **Demonstrable results** — "last-place team started hitting and winning"; "gained a lot of confidence, mechanics improved" (Millikan HS player on d'Arnaud). ([Long Beach Current](https://lbcurrent.com/sports/2025/02/14/darnaud-athletics-the-baseball-academy-gives-back-to-the-community/))
3. **Scheduling flexibility** — explicitly praised (Diane Lewis "very flexible scheduling… understands practice and game days"). ([Yelp/lessons](https://www.yelp.com/search?find_desc=Baseball+Training&find_loc=Long+Beach%2C+CA))
4. **Safety / credentials / trust** — background checks + money-back guarantees are sold hard on marketplaces (CoachUp). ([CoachUp](https://www.coachup.com/sports/long-beach-ca/baseball))
5. **Price-to-value** — the free first lesson and "Best Price" tags drive first contact.
6. **Reputation / reviews** — star ratings, review counts, and platform response-rate badges.

**Implication for the site:** the homepage must surface (in this order) **a free-first-lesson CTA**, **the coach's story/credentials**, **social proof / results**, and **easy scheduling**. Testimonials and before/after results are high-leverage once collected.
