## 1. Voice & Style Guide

**Core voice (one line):** Old-school accountability meets modern pitch design — fast, rep-driven,
demanding, relentlessly encouraging. He coaches by name, in cues, never in lectures.

**Tone attributes:**

| Trait | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| Direct | "What are you doing? Come on. Focus." — holds the standard out loud. |
| Encouraging | Constant reinforcement: "Good." "Good job." "Great pitch." "That's the best pitch." |
| Technical, not vague | Every correction is a specific cue: "Elbow up, higher." "Fix your hips — squared and balanced." |
| Personal | Uses names every rep (Axel, Theo, Max). Coaches the player in front of him, not a template. |
| Calm under pressure | "You don't need to do anything outside of what you've already been doing. Your job is to execute." |

**Signature vocabulary (his actual words — use these):**
- "Establish the fastball" / "middle middle gas"
- "Built from your legs, and forward"
- "Don't aim it — rip it"
- "Your swing-and-miss pitch"
- "Natural run" / "over-grip it for more run"
- "Execute"
- "Tap in"
- "Double clutch / double hitch" (the flaw he diagnoses + fixes)
- Reps as cadence: "three more," "one more," "four back"
- "bud," "boys," "bro," "dude" (warm, locker-room)

**Sentence rhythm:** Short. Imperative. One cue at a time. He stacks small commands
("Sit back. Rip and pull. Good. Three more.") rather than explaining mechanics in paragraphs.

**Do / Don't for site copy:**
- ✅ Short, confident imperatives. "We build it from the ground up." "Establish the fastball, then expand."
- ✅ Lead with the player. "Your player," not "athletes."
- ✅ Use his real terms (swing-and-miss pitch, natural run, execute).
- ❌ No generic training-site filler ("unlock your potential," "take your game to the next level") — that's
  the exact anonymous voice the OLD site had.
- ❌ Don't over-explain. He doesn't; the copy shouldn't.

**Before / After:**
- ❌ Generic: *"Our comprehensive pitching program helps athletes develop their full arsenal and reach
  their potential."*
- ✅ Myles: *"We build your arsenal one pitch at a time — establish the fastball, then add the cutter, the
  two-seam, the changeup. Every pitch thrown like a fastball. That's how you get swing-and-miss."*

## 2. "How Myles Coaches" — Methodology Copy (site-ready draft)

*Placement TBD: a homepage credibility block, OR a dedicated `/pitching` page (recommended — it's an SEO
long-tail magnet and a clear premium service line). Decide before wiring.*

### How Coach Myles Builds a Pitcher

Myles doesn't hand kids a generic program. He reviews their game film, finds the one thing holding them
back, and fixes it rep by rep — then builds a full arsenal on top of it. Calm, accountable, ground-up.

**The arsenal** (this is the differentiator *and* the SEO) — real pitch development, taught the way a
former semi-pro actually pitched:

- **Fastball** — the foundation. Built from the legs and forward, no wasted motion (no "double-hitch").
  Establish it first, command it middle-middle, then expand.
- **Two-seam** — over-gripped for heavier run. "Don't aim it — come over the top and rip it."
- **Changeup** — same arm action as the fastball, so the hitter can't read it. Death to lower-half hitters.
- **Cutter** — thrown like a fastball, breaks late. "That's your swing-and-miss pitch."
- **Knuckle curve** — thrown like a football, more tumble, dropped right into the zone.

**The method:**
- **Film first.** "That's what I saw on your video — I'm going to help you with that." Fixes start from
  real game footage.
- **Mechanics that hold under pressure.** Squared hips, elbow up, balanced — fixing the double-clutch so
  the delivery repeats.
- **Game-rep bullpens.** Sessions mirror a real outing: establish the fastball, work away, run the
  secondary pitches, finish on a heater.
- **Accountability.** Demands focus, gives constant feedback — tough when it needs to be, encouraging
  every good rep.
