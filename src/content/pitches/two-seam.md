The two-seam is a movement fastball — same fastball intent, but with late, arm-side run that gets weak contact and soft ground balls instead of barrels.

## Proper mechanics

The two-seam is still a fastball, so the delivery doesn't change — same lower-half drive, same arm speed, same finish. The movement comes from the grip and from letting the ball run, not from twisting the wrist or trying to manufacture break. A young pitcher should think "fastball," not "trick pitch." The moment a kid tries to steer a two-seam, the velocity drops and the movement flattens right out.

## Developing it the right way

The two-seam is one of the safest secondary looks for a young arm, because it's thrown with fastball mechanics — there's none of the extra stress a breaking ball puts on the elbow. That makes it a smart next step once the four-seam fastball is commanded. Learn to throw it for strikes to the arm side first; the run shows up naturally as arm speed and conviction stay up.

## The mindset

Movement rewards aggression. A two-seam thrown with conviction runs; a two-seam that's aimed just sits there waiting to get hit. The cue is simple: **don't aim it — rip it.** Trust the grip to do the work and commit to the target.
