The knuckle curve is a "get-me-over" breaking ball — north-south depth that drops into the zone for a called strike and changes the hitter's eye level. It's the most advanced pitch in the arsenal, and the last one Coach Myles adds.

## Proper mechanics

The knuckle curve gets its depth from spin and a clean, over-the-top finish — the ball tumbles forward and drops, rather than sweeping sideways. The key is throwing it with intent and a consistent release point so it finishes in the zone; a tentative breaking ball hangs, and a hanging breaker is the most hittable pitch in baseball. The delivery and arm slot stay the same as the fastball so the hitter can't read it early.

## Developing it the right way

This is the pitch Coach Myles is most careful with. Breaking balls put more stress on a young elbow than fastballs or changeups, so the knuckle curve comes only after a pitcher has a commanded fastball and changeup, sound mechanics, and the physical maturity to handle it — consistent with Pitch Smart's guidance on introducing breaking pitches later. Rushed breaking balls are a leading cause of youth arm trouble. Taught at the right time and the right way, it's a weapon; thrown too early, it's a risk that simply isn't worth it.

## The mindset

A breaking ball demands trust. You have to commit to the spin and trust it to finish — the cue is **more tumble**, thrown with conviction, not steered into the zone. A pitcher who trusts his curveball throws it for strikes; one who doesn't, hangs it.
