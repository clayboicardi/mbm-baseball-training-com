A good changeup isn't about being slow — it's about looking exactly like the fastball until it's too late. It's one of the most valuable pitches a young pitcher can own, and one of the safest for a developing arm.

## Proper mechanics

The whole pitch lives in the arm action. The changeup has to come out of the same delivery and the same arm speed as the fastball — the grip is what takes the speed off, not a slower arm. If the arm decelerates, the hitter reads it instantly. Thrown right, the changeup mirrors the fastball out of the hand, and the hitter's timing is already gone before he realizes the ball is slower.

## Developing it the right way

Because it's thrown with fastball arm action and no wrist-twisting, the changeup puts very little extra stress on a young arm — which is exactly why Coach Myles teaches it before any breaking ball. Fastball, then changeup: that two-pitch combination, both commanded, gets a young pitcher deep into games and keeps the arm healthy while the body matures. Reps are everything here; the feel for taking speed off while keeping arm speed up only comes from throwing it a lot.

## The mindset

The changeup is a patience pitch. You have to trust it and throw it with the same intent as a fastball, even when your brain wants to baby it. The cue: **same arm action.** Sell the fastball, and let the grip do the rest.
